### Project Description
Schrödinger's Chess is a chess game where the pieces look completely normal, but their actual movement powers are randomly reassigned at the beginning of every game. A piece that looks like a Pawn might move like a Bishop, while another seemingly ordinary piece could secretly possess the power of a King.

Players know the movement powers of their own pieces, but must figure out what their opponent's pieces can actually do by observing their moves. The game supports both single-player matches against a computer and online multiplayer matches using a shared game code.

### The Problem (that doesn't exist)
Chess has become far too predictable.

You see a Pawn, so naturally you expect it to behave like a Pawn. You see a Rook, you expect a Rook. We decided this was a serious problem.

### The Solution (that nobody asked for)
We kept the familiar chess board, pieces, starting positions and appearance — and removed the one thing your brain relies on: **knowing what the pieces actually do.**

At the beginning of every game, the standard movement powers are randomly shuffled among the pieces while maintaining the normal number of each power. Your own pieces reveal their actual movement through their legal moves, while your opponent's powers remain hidden.

The computer opponent also plays under the same hidden-information concept instead of simply being given perfect knowledge of everything.

The result is chess where you know the rules, recognize every piece, and still have absolutely no idea what you're looking at.
